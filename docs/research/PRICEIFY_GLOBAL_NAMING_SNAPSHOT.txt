Key Innovations for Your New Methodology
1. Real-Time Web Search for Competitor Price Banding (Phase 1)
Instead of relying on outdated internal LLM parametric memory, the prompt instructs the model to execute targeted web searches using the Product Title + Key Specifications.


* How it works: The model searches Google Shopping / major e-commerce benchmarks to identify:
* 
   * Floor Price: The lowest-end budget alternatives.
   *    * Mid-Market Average: The standard market consensus price.
   *    * Ceiling Price: Premium/luxury tier alternatives.
   *    * Why this matters: If your merchant sells a handmade leather wallet for £25, but the mid-market average online for genuine leather wallets is £45–£60, the tool immediately identifies an under-priced growth opportunity.
   * 2. Multimodal Perceived Value Scoring (Product Image Analysis)
Sending product images directly to Vision-capable models (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet or GPT-4o) unlocks branding intelligence:


   * Packaging & Photography Quality: Is it cheap dropshipped imagery or professional lifestyle photography?
   *    * Build Quality & Aesthetic: Does the product look like a £15 impulse buy or an £80 premium item?
   *    * USP Identification: Visual callouts (e.g., organic materials, ergonomic design, luxury finishing).
   * 3. Output Quality Upgrade: "Chain of Thought" Thinking
By allowing the model to write out its structural analysis in a <thinking> tag before returning the JSON, we eliminate hallucinated numbers and ensure the output percentages directly reflect its market search findings.

Your goal is to propose realistic A/B price test ranges (% changes) to maximize overall Revenue Per Visitor (RPV) and total revenue for a given product array.

- Product Title, Description, & High-Res Product Image

### STEP-BY-STEP REASONING PROCESS
For each product, perform the following internal analysis inside a <thinking> block before generating JSON:


1. VISUAL & PERCEIVED VALUE ASSESSMENT (IMAGE & DESCRIPTION):
   - Evaluate the product's aesthetic quality, packaging, and branding from the image.
   - Identify core USPs (materials, design, utility, target market).
   - Determine its perceived positioning tier: [Budget / Entry Level | Mid-Market Standard | Premium / Luxury].


2. COMPETITOR LANDSCAPE & BENCHMARKING (WEB RESEARCH):
   - Search for comparable products online using the product title and core specs.
   - Establish the current market price range:
     * Market Low (Budget alternatives)
     * Market Average (Standard consensus price)
     * Market High (Premium equivalents)
   - Benchmark the merchant's current price against this range to determine if the item is Overpriced, Underpriced, or Fairly Positioned.


3. HISTORICAL SALES PERFORMANCE EVALUATION:
   - High/Steady Sales + Low/Fair Price -> Candidate for PRICE INCREASE (Test higher margin capture).
   - Zero/Low Sales + Price Above Market Avg -> Candidate for PRICE CUT (Test demand activation).
   - Zero/Low Sales + Price Already Below Market Low -> Do NOT cut aggressively (Issue is likely marketing/listing trust, not price).


4. PRICE ELASTICITY & BAND CALCULATION:
   - Calculate percentage bounds [lo, hi] and the expected "best" target price percentage.
   - Ensure the range stays within allowed limits: [${min}%, ${max}%].
   - Ensure (hi - lo) >= ${minBandWidth}% so test variants sit at least ${minGapHint}% apart for statistical distinction.


---


### OUTPUT FORMAT
Return strictly valid JSON inside markdown code blocks (```json ... ```) with NO other text outside.


{
  "summary": "One sentence summarizing the overall strategy for this test batch (max 200 chars).",
  "bands": [
    {
      "v": 0,
      "direction": "rise" | "cut" | "either" | "hold",
      "lo": 8,
      "hi": 18,
      "best": 14,
      "confidence": "low" | "medium" | "high",
      "market_benchmarks": {
        "market_avg_price": "45.00",
        "positioning": "underpriced_relative_to_market"
      },
      "rationale": "Clear, concise merchant explanation focusing on market gap and sales behavior (max 150 chars)."
    }
  ]
}


---


### EXECUTION RULES
1. "v" corresponds to the index of the product in the input array. Output every product exactly once.
2. Positive numbers raise price (+12 = 12% increase); negative numbers lower price (-10 = 10% discount).
3. Do not invent competitor store names or exact quote claims in the merchant rationale—keep the rationale punchy and actionable.
4. When sales history is zero/minimal, rely heavily on market benchmarks and perceived value from the image/description.`;

Mini‑glossary for the team
To keep everyone naming things the same way:
                  * Test – A single price or offer comparison you run in Priceify.
                  * Price test – Changes the actual price shown to shoppers, without a crossed‑out original.
                  * Offer test – Shows a sale price with the original price crossed out.
                  * Control – The current price your store already uses.
                  * Variation – A different price or offer you’re testing against control.
                  * Revenue per visitor – Revenue generated divided by number of visitors who saw the test.
                  * Confidence – How sure the maths is that one variation is better than another.
                  * Minimum visitors per variation – The number of visitors each variation must see before results are calculated.
                  * Price locations – The pages where prices appear (product, collection, cart, search, home).
                  * Theme selector – The CSS selector Priceify uses to find a price on your theme.[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]

3. Text generation rules
3.1 Test line
Format:
Test: {name} — {typeLabel} · {productsText} · {pricingText}
Type label:
                  * price → "Price test"
                  * offer → "Offer test"
Products text:
                  * If productsScope === 'all' →
"{productsCount} products · All products"
                  * If productsScope === 'picked' →
"{productsCount} products · Picked products"
Pricing text:
                     * ai_suggested → "AI suggested prices"
                     * bulk_adjust → "Bulk adjusted prices"
                     * manual → "Manual prices"
3.2 Traffic line
Format:
Traffic: {trafficAllocationPercent}% of eligible visitors · {variationSummary}
Variation summary:
Take variations array and join control + non‑controls:
                     * Control first:
"Control {trafficPercent}%"
                     * Each variation:
"Var A {trafficPercent}%", "Var B {trafficPercent}%", etc.
Implementation example:
const control = variations.find(v => v.isControl);
const others = variations.filter(v => !v.isControl);


const variationSummary = [
  control ? `Control ${control.trafficPercent}%` : null,
  ...others.map(v => `${v.name} ${v.trafficPercent}%`),
]
  .filter(Boolean)
  .join(' · ');
Result example:
Traffic: 100% of eligible visitors · Control 34% · Var A 33% · Var B 33%
3.3 Audience line
Format:
Audience: {visitorsText} · {devicesText} · {sourcesText} · {countriesText}
Visitors text:
                        * audienceSegment === 'all' → "All visitors"
                        * new → "New visitors"
                        * returning → "Returning visitors"
Devices text:
                        * If all three in array → "All devices"
                        * Otherwise join enabled ones, e.g. "Desktop & Mobile" / "Mobile only".
Sources text:
                        * If all sources selected → "All sources"
                        * Otherwise join, e.g. "Search & Email".
You can map TrafficSource → labels:
                        * direct → "Direct"
                        * search → "Search"
                        * social → "Social"
                        * email → "Email"
                        * paid_ads → "Paid ads"
                        * referral → "Referral"
Countries text:
                        * countriesMode === 'all' → "All countries"
                        * include → "Include: {countryList}"
                        * exclude → "Exclude: {countryList}"
Where countryList is “UK” / “US, CA” etc.
Example:
Audience: All visitors · All devices · All sources · All countries
3.4 Results line
Format:
Results: Primary: {primaryLabel} · {confidenceLevelPercent}% confidence · {minimumVisitorsPerVariation} visitors/variation
Primary label:
                        * revenue_per_visitor → "Revenue per visitor"
                        * conversion_rate → "Conversion rate"
                        * aov → "Average order value"
Example:
Results: Primary: Revenue per visitor · 90% confidence · 5,000 visitors/variation
3.5 Safety line
Format when guardrail ON:
Safety: Guardrail ON · Pause if Rev/visitor drops >{thresholdPercent}% vs control, after {minVisitorsPerVariation} visitors/variation
Use guardrail.thresholdPercent and guardrail.minVisitorsPerVariation.
Format when guardrail OFF:
Safety: Guardrail OFF
Example:
Safety: Guardrail ON · Pause if Rev/visitor drops >10% vs control, after 1,000 visitors/variation
(or whatever default you set).

4. History tab – “Test history”
This is the renamed and refined Activity tab.[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]
Naming
                                                * Tab label: History
                                                * Page title: Test history
                                                * Subtitle: Log of guardrail events and test changes.
Filters
                                                * Filter chips at top:
                                                * All (default)
                                                * Lifecycle (created, launched, paused, resumed, stopped, winners applied)
                                                * Guardrails (guardrail stops, exclusions, threshold changes)
Event row contents
Each row includes:
                                                1. Icon: coloured dot (green/ yellow/ red) indicating type.
                                                2. Title (bold), examples:
                                                * Stopped by guardrail
                                                * Test paused
                                                * Test resumed
                                                * Launched test
                                                * Created test
                                                * Guardrail excluded product
                                                * Winner applied to catalog
                                                3. Meta line: “actor — date, time”, e.g.
                                                * You — Sep 3, 2026, 11:25 AM
                                                * Guardrail — Sep 3, 2026, 11:25 AM
                                                4. Detail text:
                                                * Guardrail stop example:
Revenue per visitor dropped 34.1% vs control (limit 10%). Traffic assignment stopped.
                                                * Product exclusion example:
Guardrail excluded “Gift Card” from the test. Revenue per visitor fell below the safety limit.
                                                * Test launch example:
Traffic allocation set to 50%. All visitors in your audience segment can enter this test.
                                                * Winner applied example:
Applied winner “Variation B” for “Gift Card”. Catalog price updated.
                                                   5. Badge at right indicating event type:
                                                   * Guardrail / Paused / Launched / Created / Winner applied / Settings changed.
Event types to log
                                                   * Lifecycle:
                                                   * Created test
                                                   * Launched test
                                                   * Paused test
                                                   * Resumed test
                                                   * Stopped test
                                                   * Winner applied (single product)
                                                   * Winners applied in bulk
                                                   * Guardrail:
                                                   * Guardrail paused test (global)
                                                   * Guardrail resumed test (if applicable)
                                                   * Guardrail excluded product(s)
                                                   * Guardrail threshold changed
                                                   * Settings changes:
                                                   * Audience targeting changed
                                                   * Traffic allocation changed
                                                   * Primary metric changed
                                                   * Guardrail turned ON/OFF
